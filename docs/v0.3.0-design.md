# humanoid_parkour v0.3.0 — advanced parkour design

**Status: design for review. No implementation yet.**

The goal is not "a harder version of v0.2.0". The goal is a competition whose **playbacks are
worth watching** — where a converged field is visibly doing athletic, whole-body parkour, and where
a viewer can tell a great run from a good one without reading a number.

This document says what to build, what it costs, and where the risks are.

## 1. Why v0.2.0 cannot get there

Measured, 960 course instances through the released referee image (8 seeds × 120):

| Tier | Completion | Mean instance score | Non-completion median progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| easy | 38.4% | 1.014 | 0.667 |
| medium | 10.3% | 0.654 | 0.550 |
| hard | **0.0%** | 0.390 | 0.380 |

Also: **zero timeouts, zero physics glitches, 6 out-of-bounds in 960 runs.** Every course either
completes in ~365 steps or falls early.

The structural problems:

- **Flat plane, one global friction value, full-width un-clippable hurdles**, tallest obstacle
  0.35 m against a 1.4 m torso. It is walking, not parkour.
- **The whole competition is one skill.** 1.20 of the missing score sits on the hard tier alone, and
  it is all "learn a deliberate high-step". A miner who cracks it jumps 0.69 → ~1.5 in a single
  submission (~85 takeovers' worth), after which the remaining game is grinding a 20 m sprint from
  1.5 → 1.65 against σ_round ≈ 0.03–0.04. Noise.
- **Nothing can produce a playback.** The referee emits summary stats only; there is no trajectory
  data and no renderer anywhere in the repo.

1-D is *not* the problem — Atlas and the published legged/humanoid parkour results are essentially
linear courses. Obstacle amplitude, maneuver variety, and arm use are the problem.

## 2. Robot: Unitree G1 (verified against `mujoco_menagerie`)

`google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/unitree_g1`, derived from Unitree's `g1_29dof_rev_1_0`.

- **29 DoF.** Variants: `g1.xml`, `g1_with_hands.xml` (adds finger articulation),
  `g1_mjx.xml` + `scene_mjx.xml`.
- **Build on the `g1_mjx.xml` lineage.** It has hand-designed collision geoms and contact pairs,
  tuned solver/line-search iterations, lower and more realistic PD gains, and — the reason it
  matters — it was **successfully transferred to hardware** in MuJoCo Playground. That is as close
  to a validated humanoid as we will get for free, and using the *same* XML for miner training
  (MJX/GPU) and evaluation (CPU MuJoCo) is exactly what we want for score reproducibility.
- **Skip `g1_with_hands.xml`.** Finger articulation is not needed: "hands allowed" (§4) means
  forearm/wrist *geom contact* for vaulting and climbing, not grasping. Fewer DoF, much easier
  training.
- **Licence**: Unitree's own BSD-3-style redistribution licence, not Apache. Permissive, but it has
  conditions — read it in full and vendor the notice before shipping.

**The action space changes.** Menagerie's G1 uses **joint-position actuators** ("needs tuning", per
its README), not the torque actuators v0.2.0 used. So `action` becomes **target joint positions**
fed to a PD controller. This is the modern standard for legged RL and is easier to learn than raw
torque — but it means `ACT_DIM` goes 17 → ~29 and the PD gains become part of the competition's
pinned, score-affecting configuration.

**Costs, stated plainly**: `baseline/baseline.onnx` is invalidated and must be retrained from
scratch; `OBS_DIM`/`ACT_DIM` both change; `env/assets_humanoid.xml` is replaced. This is a new
competition wearing the same id.

### 2.1 Measured model facts

Loaded from `mujoco_menagerie/unitree_g1` with MuJoCo 3.10.0:

| | `scene.xml` | `scene_mjx.xml` |
|---|---|---|
| DoF | nq 36 / nv 35 / **nu 29** | same |
| mass | **33.3 kg** | same |
| pelvis height standing | 0.790 m | 0.784 m |
| robot height | ~1.22 m | ~1.26 m |
| solver | 100 iter, ls 50, dt 0.002 | **5 iter, ls 8, dt 0.004** |
| PD gains | **kp 500 uniform (untuned)** | **kp 20–75 per joint, kv 2** |

`scene.xml`'s uniform kp=500 is the "needs tuning" placeholder the README warns about — another
reason the mjx lineage is the right base.

### 2.2 Contact-fidelity validation — **passed, with one caveat**

The open risk was that 5 solver iterations would let a policy extract free energy from contacts.
Tested directly:

- **Sustained energy pumping** (sinusoidal joint oscillation while standing, 2–50 Hz, 4 s): energy
  gain is **identical across 5, 20, and 50 iterations** (e.g. 39.8 J at 2 Hz in all three), and peak
  COM rise is 2.3 cm in all three. The gain is the PD actuators doing legitimate work, not a solver
  leak. **No sustained exploit vector from the low iteration count.**
- **Passive drop** (actuation disabled, so energy must be non-increasing; onto plane and onto a box):
  net energy is strongly dissipative everywhere (−250 to −337 J). But the **worst single-step energy
  rise is 8.3 J at 5 iterations vs 2.1–2.6 J at 20–50** — a real ~4× transient artifact at impact.
  For a 33.3 kg robot, 8.3 J ≈ 2.5 cm of lift, per impact event, non-repeatable.
- **Penetration is ~1.6–2.3 cm on every configuration**, essentially independent of iteration count —
  so it is a contact-softness (`solref`/`solimp`) property, not a solver-iteration one. On a 0.15 m
  stair rise that is ~13% geometric error.

**Verdict: the mjx settings are usable.** Keep the solver-exploitation gate (§7) as defence in depth
rather than as the primary mitigation, and **stiffen `solref` on terrain geoms** to pull penetration
down before it distorts stair and platform contact.

### 2.3 Reach is not the binding constraint

Foot height reachable with the pelvis held at standing height:

| Posture | Foot z |
|---|---|
| standing | 0.005 m |
| moderate step (hip 45°, knee 60°) | **0.452 m** |
| high knee (hip 90°, knee 90°) | 1.005 m |
| kinematic max (hip 165°) | 1.331 m |

So a **0.45 m platform is a *moderate* step for G1**, and the proposed 0.45–0.75 m climb-ups sit
well inside kinematic reach. What limits climbing is balance, torque, and learnability — not
geometry.

This also settles the sizing question in the other direction: **v0.2.0's hardest 0.35 m hurdle is
below a moderate step for G1**, so the entire old difficulty ladder becomes trivial on this robot.
The obstacle set must scale up, exactly as §3 proposes.

### 2.4 On-ramp calibrated against the stock G1 walker

Unitree ships a pretrained G1 walking policy (`unitree_rl_gym`, `deploy/pre_train/g1/motion.pt`:
47-dim obs, **12 actuated DoF, legs only**, 50 Hz, position-offset actions). It is a flat-ground
velocity tracker that has never seen terrain, which makes it a good probe for "what can a naive
policy do?" — and the intended stand-in for the v1 baseline. Driven over the course
(`tools/preview_v3.py --walk`):

- **It needs heading hold.** `motion.pt` tracks *body-frame* velocity with no heading feedback, so
  yaw error integrates into **0.26 m of lateral drift per metre travelled** — it walks off any
  finite-width track long before reaching an obstacle. Closing that loop (steer back to the
  centreline, as any real deployment does) cut drift from **5.63 m to 0.01 m**. **The baseline
  wrapper must include this**, or it scores near zero for reasons unrelated to parkour.
  This was initially misdiagnosed as the walker failing a 0.10 m step; the giveaway was a 0° flat
  control case failing at the identical distance.
- **Climb limit is between 15.4° and 20.1°.** It clears 0.55 m over 2.0 m; it stalls on the slope
  at 0.55 m over 1.5 m. The on-ramp is set at **15.4°**, right at that ceiling.
- **Drop height is nearly free.** A 0.20 m drop and a 0.55 m drop both end its run at the same
  place (23% progress either way) — a flat-ground walker has no landing controller, so any drop
  destabilises it equally. Since the cost is identical, the on-ramp takes the **full 0.55 m sheer
  drop** for the spectacle.
- **Resulting baseline: 21% progress** (10.6 m of 51.1 m), dying on the on-ramp's landing. That is
  a legitimate `baseline_raw_score` — non-zero, non-trivial, with the entry bar set at "absorb a
  0.55 m landing".

Caveat: this is the 12-DoF legs-only robot, not the 29-DoF Menagerie G1 the competition will use.
It calibrates *difficulty*, and does not transfer as-is.

## 3. Obstacle set — built for spectacle

A course is an ordered sequence of **segments** (51.1 m in the current prototype, including a 12 m on-ramp), each with its own geometry and a
hidden friction value. Sized for a ~1.3 m robot:

| Maneuver | Geometry | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| **climb-up** | platform 0.45–0.75 m (hip-to-chest) | the iconic move; impossible without arms |
| **drop-down** | descent 0.5–1.0 m | landing control; visually dramatic |
| **leap** | gap 0.6–1.4 m | airborne, unmistakable |
| **vault** | waist-height barrier, no gap behind | pure arm-assisted athleticism |
| **crawl-under** | overhead bar at 0.6–0.9 m | forces a whole-body posture change — the cheapest way to get a *visually distinct* behavior |
| **balance-traverse** | beam 0.25–0.4 m wide | slow and tense; legible skill |
| **stairs up/down** | rise 0.15–0.25, run 0.25–0.40 | connective tissue; descent is the harder half |
| **slick patch** | μ 0.15–0.35, geometry identical to flat | the invisible skill; produces recoveries |

**Geometry: composited box geoms, not an `hfield`.** Boxes give exact edges for stairs and
platforms, and let the height scan (§5) be computed **analytically** from the segment list rather
than by raycasting — which keeps terrain sensing out of our already-tight determinism budget.
`rubble`, if added later, is the one case that wants a small `hfield`.

**Gaps and drop-downs need the course raised**, so the track sits on a plinth (top ≈ 0.8 m) and
falling off becomes a new terminal reason `fell_off`.

Stays **1-D** per your call: no lateral route choice, obstacles span the track width.

## 4. Hands: allowed on obstacles, not on the ground

The highest-leverage change for spectacle. Vaulting and climbing are the moves people screenshot,
and they need arms.

**Rule:** any geom may contact **obstacle** geoms freely. Non-foot contact with the **ground plane**
still terminates the episode as `fell`.

That permits vault, climb-up, and hand-assisted landings while forbidding the degenerate
bear-crawl-the-whole-course strategy, which would look terrible and would otherwise win.

**This is a deliberate revision of the §1 goal statement**, which currently says "the only part of
the robot used for locomotion is the legs". The new statement should say something like: *athletic,
committed, whole-body parkour — arms used the way a human parkour athlete uses them, not as a
substitute for legs.* Needs writing before implementation, because §7's gates derive from it.

## 5. Observation — geometry visible, surfaces hidden

Per your earlier call, friction stays hidden, which is what makes this an adaptation problem and
forces memory (§6).

| Block | Dims | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| projected gravity | 3 | replaces raw quaternion; no sign ambiguity |
| base height above **local** terrain | 1 | absolute z cannot generalize over elevation |
| joint positions | 29 | |
| joint velocities | 29 | |
| base linear velocity | 3 | |
| base angular velocity | 3 | |
| previous action | 29 | standard; implicit dynamics feedback |
| foot contact flags | 2 | main channel for inferring friction |
| **height scan** | **77** | 11 (x) × 7 (y) grid in the base yaw frame, x ∈ [−0.5, 2.5] m, y ∈ [−0.6, 0.6] m; values = `base_z − terrain_height` |
| **overhead clearance scan** | **11** | headroom above the robot along x — **required** for crawl-under to be solvable at all |
| task | 2 | fraction of course remaining, lateral offset |

≈ **189 dims.** Explicitly absent: friction, segment class labels, segment parameters, the course
seed. The scans are the only exteroception — the `legged_gym`/ANYmal formulation that made
perceptive locomotion work.

Note the overhead scan is not optional: with a purely downward height scan, an overhead bar is
invisible and crawl-under becomes an unlearnable random death.

This also removes the v0.2.0 seed leak by construction — with geometry delivered as a scan, there is
never a reason to send a seed or index into the player sandbox.

## 6. Submission contract — ONNX, with optional recurrence

**ONNX stays.** SB3 is a training framework, not a submission format; its native artifact is a
pickle `.zip` — arbitrary code execution at load. Accepting those drops us from the top of the
skill's format ladder to the bottom and forces a Layer-2 screen we currently don't need. Miners can
train in anything; only the graph is submitted.

The current fixed `[1,56] → [1,17]` interface forbids memory, and hidden friction makes memory
close to mandatory. New contract accepts **either**:

```
feed-forward:  obs [batch, 189]                    -> action [batch, 29]
recurrent:     obs [batch, 189], h_in [batch, H]   -> action [batch, 29], h_out [batch, H]
```

`H` declared in the spec (suggest 128). The player wrapper zeroes `h` on `reset()` and feeds `h_out`
back each step. One interface, recurrence optional.

Consequence: `reset()` stops being a no-op, so the wrapper now holds per-episode state and **must**
clear it — otherwise state leaks across courses and scores become order-dependent.

## 7. Gates, revised

- `fell`: torso below a **terrain-relative** height, or non-foot contact with the **ground plane**
  (obstacle contact allowed, per §4).
- `fell_off`: new — off the plinth or into a gap.
- `physics_glitch`: v0.2.0's `|qvel| > 100` is ~30× the baseline's 3.5 m/s and bounds nothing.
  Replace with per-body plausible-dynamics limits, and **reorder so `fell`/`fell_off` are checked
  before `completed`** — today a ballistic body crossing the line mid-fall scores a full completion.
- **Solver-exploitation gate**: new and important given §2's open risk. Track contact impulse
  magnitudes and reject runs that extract implausible energy from the solver. Contact-rich climbing
  makes this a live threat rather than a theoretical one.

## 8. Scoring — checkpoints

~20 checkpoints along the course; score credits checkpoints reached plus a time bonus on completion.
Keep the ordering property (any completion beats any non-completion; faster beats slower).

The reason is structural, not cosmetic. v0.2.0's score is nearly **bimodal** — complete (1.0–2.0) or
fall (0.0–0.3) — so a single coin-flip moves the mean hard, which is why σ_round is 21× the sizing
criterion. A 20-checkpoint score is far smoother. **This is the most plausible route to actually
passing the criterion** instead of asking the platform to work around it. Stratify N across segment
classes on top.

Progress stays monotone in x, so no loop-farming.

## 9. Evaluation cost — batch the observations

Prerequisite for all of the above, because richer terrain makes it worse. v0.2.0 issues
`N × steps` ≈ 108,000 sequential `/act` calls inside `referee.timeout_s: 900` — ~8 ms per call
against a permitted `deadline_ms: 500`. A 40 m course at ~1800 steps would be far worse.

**Step all active courses in lockstep and send one `/act` per timestep with a `[K, OBS]` batch.**
Round-trips drop from `N × steps` to `steps` — ~120×, removing minutes of pure protocol overhead.
~120 small `MjModel`/`MjData` pairs is tens of MB, fine inside 1.5Gi.

**Determinism constraint that decides the implementation:** batched and single-row matmul do not
produce identical floats, and the active set shrinks as courses terminate, so batch width varies
mid-episode. The player wrapper must therefore **loop over batch rows internally**, running the
graph one row at a time. We take the HTTP amortization and refuse the numerical variability — and a
miner's fixed-batch-1 export keeps working.

Worth landing in v0.2.x independently of this redesign.

## 10. Playback — the deliverable that makes it "cook"

Currently impossible; nothing captures trajectories. Needed:

- **Referee emits `qpos` traces** for a bounded selection — the top-K runs plus one representative
  per segment class — subsampled (e.g. every 3rd control step). Full traces for 120 courses are far
  too large for `result.json`.
- **A `tools/render.py`** that replays a trace against the same MJCF and writes MP4 via MuJoCo's
  renderer. Runs offline, outside the sandbox.
- Traces go in post-round-reveal metadata, so nothing leaks mid-round.

Cheap, and without it the competition produces numbers instead of highlight reels.

## 11. The ceiling problem (deferred, pending a platform answer)

A solo absolute metric has a ceiling by construction: however rich the course, a converged field
completes it and the leaderboard becomes σ-dominated. Better obstacles raise the ceiling; they do
not remove it.

**Adaptive difficulty** via `entrypoints.generate_round` — scaling terrain difficulty with the
current top score so the ceiling recedes — is the fix, but it **only works if the platform re-scores
the incumbent leader on each round's input.** Otherwise the 1% takeover rule compares scores drawn
from different difficulty distributions and is meaningless.

Per your call: build **fixed difficulty tiers**, structured so adaptive difficulty can be switched
on later, and ask the platform team whether incumbent re-scoring exists. Same question as sizing
open item 1, so one answer unlocks both.

Longer term, an adversarial format (humanoid platform fighting) removes the ceiling permanently.
Sequencing note: the robot, perception stack, recurrent contract, batched referee, and playback
tooling built here **all transfer** to that competition — this is the cheaper way to build that
foundation, and duels carry design costs (mirrored fair matches, `swap_sides`, deterministic
tiebreaks, forfeits, plus degenerate-strategy risks like edge-camping and mutual falls) best paid
after the fundamentals are proven.

## 12. Baseline feasibility — the biggest risk

Contact-rich humanoid parkour with hidden friction is at the research frontier. Published results
use ~1e9–1e10 environment steps with massively parallel GPU simulation. **CPU MuJoCo + SB3 will not
get there** — v0.2.0's baseline took ~110M steps and still fails 83% of a far easier task.

- Train on **MJX (GPU)** using the `g1_mjx.xml` lineage, with a curriculum over segment classes and
  domain randomization over friction and PD gains.
- Evaluation stays `process_type: cpu`; GPU is an off-platform *baseline training* cost and does not
  change the spec.
- **Keep the easy tier comfortably solvable** so the baseline scores well clear of 0 and miners have
  a climbable gradient. If the baseline cannot clear the easy tier, the competition is noise.
- Miner compute cost rises substantially, which interacts with the ≈$1 fee and 5-day reveal. Both
  want revisiting.

## 13. Order of work

1. **Batched-lockstep referee** (§9) on v0.2.x — independent, unblocks everything.
2. **Vendor G1**, validate contact fidelity and solver settings for climbing/vaulting (§2 open
   risk), settle PD gains. Blocks all dimensioning.
3. **Rewrite the goal statement** for arms-allowed athletic parkour (§4) — the gates derive from it.
4. **Segment composer + analytic height/overhead scans** (§3, §5), with a render script from day one
   so terrain is reviewed visually, not by unit test.
5. **New submission contract + player wrapper** (§6), including per-episode state reset.
6. **Checkpoint scoring + revised gates + solver-exploitation gate** (§7, §8), then **re-measure
   σ_round** — the number that decides whether the design is sound.
7. **Playback capture + `tools/render.py`** (§10).
8. **Baseline on MJX** (§12); iterate difficulty until the easy tier is comfortably solvable.
9. Re-run sizing, rewrite HANDOFF §4, release v0.3.0.

## 14. Open decisions

1. **Crawl-under**: keep? It is the best visual-variety-per-unit-effort item but adds the overhead
   scan (11 dims) and a posture skill that may be hard for G1's kinematics.
2. **PD gains**: fixed by us (fair, simpler) or miner-selectable within limits (more expressive,
   but becomes part of the submission and needs validation)?
3. **Solver iterations** for the CPU referee: match `g1_mjx.xml` for train/eval parity, or raise for
   contact fidelity and accept the wall-clock cost?
4. **Course length**: 40 m as proposed, or longer with more segments per run for a smoother
   checkpoint score?
